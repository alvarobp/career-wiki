# The End of the Product

**Source:** [El fin del producto](https://tramontana.ai/fin-producto) - Javier Cañada (Instituto Tramontana)

## Summary

Javier Cañada argues that AI is not just changing interfaces. It is removing the reason digital products exist. Service companies used to stand out because of their **costly operations**: millions of human hours of process work that competitors couldn't easily copy. AI agents now do that work in tens of machine hours, so **"the monopoly of operations is broken."** A product is a *standardised* way of meeting a need. Once agents can build a solution fitted to each person's context, standardisation stops paying for itself. What dies is **intermediation**, the layer that digital product designers built between people and the functions they want. The interface itself survives.

He sets this inside a historical pattern. When a technology changes energy, production and communication all at once, it changes civilisation within about thirty years, as happened between 1890 and 1920. For designers and schools that means today's market, profession and curriculum may not exist in thirty years. He offers no plan, only a question: *"If you started from scratch today, what would you keep?"* His career advice is just as blunt: run.

## The Intermediate Layer

- Digital product designers build **the layer between a person and what they want done**, the interface between person and function.
- Most of these are *new interfaces for old functions*: travel, shopping, moving money.
- Cañada's earlier podcast, *El fin de la interfaz*, argued that AI was turning graphical interfaces into conversational ones. This essay goes further: the change isn't GUI to chat, it's that the intermediary stops being needed at all.

## The Tip of the Iceberg: Capital, Operations, Legal Framework

- A service company comes down to three things: **capital, operations and legal framework**.
- The visible product is only the tip of the iceberg. The real moat was the operations underneath it.
- Operations used to take **millions of human hours** and now start to take **tens of machine hours**, so the moat is gone.
- **The legal framework won't save incumbents.** The incumbents write it themselves. A Google lawyer in Brussels showed him a draft EU directive and said, "We write them for them."
- A CEO told him that AI already does half of what her company does for clients. In his words, "We are scared."

## Why the Product Dies

- **Product = standardisation.** It is one packaged answer that everyone has to fit.
- AI agents can build **solutions fitted to each person's context**, so the person no longer has to adapt to the product.
- With standardisation gone, the digital product loses its reason to exist. The real casualty is **intermediation, not the interface**.

## A Programme Is a Product: Schools Too

- Education has the same shape: a **programme** is a standard product, and every student is asked to fit it.
- That puts schools, including his own, in the same position as the frightened CEO.
- Tramontana's slogan promises to train professionals who will "improve the market." Cañada asks: *which market, in 30 years?* Neither schools nor digital design may still exist.
- His team's answer to "what would Tramontana be if it started from scratch?" is a prototype called **PULSO**. People teach and learn continuously, knowledge widens instead of narrowing into a fixed curriculum, and an AI amplifies and connects it.

## The Thirty-Year Civilisational Shift (Vienna, 1890)

- Between **1890 and 1920**, electricity, cinema, radio, the car, flight and mass production changed civilisation.
- The rule he draws from it: **when a technology changes energy, production and communication at once, it changes civilisation, and it takes about thirty years.**
- AI fits that pattern, so plans built on today's market are built on ground that is about to move.
- **Better carriages:** designers are losing their jobs while the field still debates Figma and design systems. He compares this to people in 1890 Vienna arguing over carriage upholstery while the car arrived. His satirical death notice for digital design "no longer seems funny" to him.

## The Question and the Advice

- **"If you started from scratch today, what would you keep?"** This was all he could give the CEO. It works as a test for any company, school or career built on a standardised offering.
- **Run.** To people asking about their careers: run in any direction, and don't save energy for the way back, because there will be nowhere to return to.

## Core Insight

AI is a threat to products because it removes the **economics of standardisation and intermediation**, not because it makes them easier to build. Operations were the moat, and agents drain it. Personalised solutions then make the one-size-fits-all product, and the one-size-fits-all programme, unnecessary. For anyone whose job is to design or teach the intermediate layer, the useful move is to stop polishing the carriage. Ask what you would keep if you started from scratch today, and commit to moving before the thirty-year shift finishes.
