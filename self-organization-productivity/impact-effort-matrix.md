# Impact-Effort Matrix

**Source:** [A smarter way to build momentum](https://buttondown.com/untools/archive/a-smarter-way-to-build-momentum/) - Tobe (Untools), June 29, 2026

## Summary

The **Impact-Effort Matrix** is a 2x2 grid for setting priorities. It scores each action on two things: how much **impact** it will have and how much **effort** it takes. The resulting four quadrants tell you what to do first, what to plan, what to save for spare time and what to avoid. Its main lesson goes against what most people do by default. Most people start with the heavy, ambitious work, but the matrix says to start with **quick wins**: the high-impact, low-effort actions.

The reason is motivational as well as practical. When progress stalls, the cause is often not laziness but effort pointed at the wrong place. Quick wins are progress by design, and the momentum they build makes the bigger projects easier to start. Picking the right task is only half the job, though. To follow through, the matrix works best alongside an [Implementation Intention](./implementation-intentions.md) that sets in advance when and where you'll act.

## The Four Quadrants

| | **Low effort** | **High effort** |
|---|---|---|
| **High impact** | Quick wins | Major projects |
| **Low impact** | Fill-ins | Thankless tasks |

- **Quick wins (high impact, low effort):** the best return on effort, so **do them first**.
  - e.g. small improvements that noticeably improve the user experience, or simple fixes that prevent bigger problems later
- **Major projects (high impact, high effort):** important, but they need significant time and resources. **Plan and execute them after the quick wins.**
- **Fill-ins (low impact, low effort):** easy, but they won't change much. **Save them for spare time.**
  - e.g. routine admin, small optimisations, minor updates
- **Thankless tasks (low impact, high effort):** a lot of effort for very little return. **Avoid or minimise them.**
  - e.g. overly complex processes that could be simplified

**The common failure:** people gravitate to major projects and thankless tasks, the "heavy stuff". They lose confidence when they can't follow through, and conclude that they lack effort when the real problem is where the effort went.

## Why Quick Wins Build Momentum

- **The progress principle:** Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer studied thousands of diary entries from workers. One factor consistently drove motivation: **making progress on meaningful work, even small progress**.
- Quick wins are the actions most likely to move something forward with the least resistance.
- Each small win creates a **feeling of momentum** that makes the next step seem possible.
- Starting with quick wins isn't taking the easy way out. It **builds the confidence** to take on the major projects.

## Pairing It with an Implementation Intention

Finding the quick wins doesn't get them done. Good intentions tend to fail at the moment of decision, when willpower runs out. Here is how to close that gap:

1. Use the matrix to find the **quick wins already on your list**.
2. Attach an [Implementation Intention](./implementation-intentions.md) to the most important one: *"When [situation], I will [behaviour]."*
   - e.g. "When I sit down at my desk on Monday morning, I will spend 20 minutes on my top quick win before opening emails."
3. The situation then triggers the behaviour. You don't depend on motivation because the decision has already been made.

## Core Insight

Stalling usually has two causes: **not knowing where to start** and **not following through when the moment arrives**. The Impact-Effort Matrix deals with the first by pointing you at high-impact, low-effort work. Small, visible progress on meaningful work fuels motivation more reliably than ambition does. An implementation intention deals with the second. Start with quick wins, deliberately and on a schedule, and use the momentum they build to carry you into the major projects.
