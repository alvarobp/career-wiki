# Implementation Intentions

**Source:** [A smarter way to build momentum](https://buttondown.com/untools/archive/a-smarter-way-to-build-momentum/) - Tobe (Untools), June 29, 2026

## Summary

Good intentions usually fail at the moment you have to act. When that moment comes, willpower runs out and the task slips. Psychologist **Peter Gollwitzer** found a simple fix: form an **implementation intention**, which means deciding in advance exactly *when* and *where* you'll act, written as "When [situation], I will [behaviour]."

With the decision already made, you don't have to rely on motivation in the moment. The situation becomes the trigger and the behaviour follows automatically. Untools pairs this with the [Impact-Effort Matrix](./impact-effort-matrix.md). The matrix tells you *what* to do first, and the implementation intention makes sure you actually do it.

## The Problem It Solves

- Plans tend to fail at the **moment of decision**, not while you're planning.
- If you leave it to in-the-moment willpower, every task means deciding all over again, and willpower is limited.
- People stall for two reasons: **not knowing where to start**, and **not following through when the moment arrives**. Implementation intentions deal with the second one.

## The Technique

**Formula:** "When [situation], I will [behaviour]."

- **Situation**: a specific cue tied to a time and place, something you'll clearly notice when it happens.
- **Behaviour**: a concrete, bounded action, not a vague goal.
- **Example**: "When I sit down at my desk on Monday morning, I will spend 20 minutes on my top quick win before opening emails."
  - The cue is concrete: sitting down at the desk on Monday morning.
  - The action has a time limit: 20 minutes.
  - It goes ahead of a competing habit: before opening emails.

## Why It Works

- **The decision is already made.** Nothing is left to weigh up when the moment comes.
- **The situation triggers the behaviour.** The cue does the job that motivation would otherwise have to do.
- **You don't rely on motivation**, so the plan still works on low-energy days.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. Find your highest-leverage next action. With the [Impact-Effort Matrix](./impact-effort-matrix.md), that's your most important **quick win**.
2. Attach an implementation intention to it by choosing the exact when and where.
3. Choose a cue that already happens reliably in your day, like arriving at your desk or finishing a meeting.

## Core Insight

Follow-through is mostly a design problem, not a matter of willpower. If you decide ahead of time and tie the action to a specific situation, the hard part is over before the moment arrives. Starting then becomes a reflex instead of a choice you have to make.
